Poland is warming faster than the world. What does the data say?

Anna Novak14 November 2026, 09:126 min read

Milder winters, July droughts and record-warm seas. Climate change is in the news almost every day. We checked which of the most repeated claims hold up against the measurements.

The winter that never comes

Winters in Poland are getting warmer, and the number of frost days has dropped sharply in recent decades. Forecasters admit that predicting a white Christmas has become a lottery.

City dwellers notice it most. Sledges and skates stay in the basement, and councils that used to stockpile road salt now plan their budgets more carefully.

The global trend

Global average temperature has risen by about 1.2°C since pre-industrial times, according to NASA and NOAA measurements. Europe is warming even faster than the global average, and summer 2024 was the hottest on record in Europe.

When I started teaching, a warm December was a curiosity. Now my students ask about it in every lesson.

The main driver is the rising concentration of greenhouse gases. Atmospheric CO2 has passed 420 ppm and keeps rising every year.

Seas and ice

The change is just as visible in the north. Arctic sea ice is shrinking every decade, and its September extent is now about 40 percent smaller than in the 1980s.

The seas are warming too. The Mediterranean Sea keeps breaking temperature records, and sea level in the Baltic is rising, as tide gauges in Świnoujście and Gdańsk show.

Drought and downpours

Farmers point to a different problem. Rainfall in Poland is becoming more irregular, and summer droughts happen more often than they used to, hurting harvests in many regions.

Hydrologists say that long dry spells followed by sudden downpours are worse for the soil than steady rain, even when the yearly total stays about the same.
